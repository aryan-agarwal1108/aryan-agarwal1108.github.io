## The question

When polarized light travels through air, scattering off molecules and particles scrambles some of its polarization. This is called depolarization. How much happens depends on the size of whatever the light hits compared with its wavelength. For my IB thesis, I looked at how changing the wavelength changes the amount of depolarization.

## Where it connects

It's basic scattering physics (Rayleigh and Mie scattering), but it matters for anything that sends polarized light through the air, from remote sensing to free-space optical and quantum communication links. That's the same area as my detector work at TII.
