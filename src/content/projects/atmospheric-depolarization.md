## Overview

When polarized light passes through the atmosphere, scattering by molecules and particles scrambles part of its polarization. This is called **depolarization**. How strong the effect is depends on the size of the scatterers relative to the **wavelength** of the light.

For my International Baccalaureate thesis, I investigated **the effect of varying wavelengths of light on depolarization due to atmospheric scattering**.

## Why it matters

The question links basic scattering physics (Rayleigh and Mie scattering) with practical systems that rely on polarized light passing through air, from remote sensing to free-space optical and quantum communication links.
