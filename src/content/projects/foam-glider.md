## The goal

We built a high-aspect-ratio glider, which just means the wing is long and skinny compared with its width. Long, thin wings make less induced drag (the drag you pay for making lift), so the glider travels further for every meter of height it loses.

## What we did

- Worked out the wingspan, chord, fuselage, and tail sizes from **measurements and calculations**
- Calculated the **weight distribution** to put the center of gravity in the right spot
- Built it from **foam**, **balsa wood**, and **epoxy**

## Getting the balance right

A glider is only stable in pitch if its center of gravity sits ahead of its neutral point. Too far forward and it noses down and loses range. Too far back and it pitches up, stalls, and tumbles. So a lot of the work was figuring out where each part's weight sits and arranging the layout to match.
