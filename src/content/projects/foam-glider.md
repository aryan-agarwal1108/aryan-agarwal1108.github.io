## Overview

In a team, I built a **high-aspect-ratio glider**. Aspect ratio is a wing's span compared with its chord. A long, slender wing creates less induced drag (the drag that comes from making lift), so a high-aspect-ratio glider can travel further for each meter of height it loses.

## What we did

- Designed the glider from **measured and calculated lengths**: wingspan, chord, fuselage, and tail sizing
- Worked out the **weight distribution** to place the center of gravity correctly
- Built the airframe from **foam**, **balsa wood**, and **epoxy**

## Why weight distribution matters

A glider is only stable in pitch if its **center of gravity sits ahead of the neutral point**. Too far forward and it noses down and loses range; too far back and it pitches up, stalls, and becomes uncontrollable. Calculating where each component's mass sits, and adjusting the layout to match, is what makes the difference between a glider that flies and one that tumbles.
